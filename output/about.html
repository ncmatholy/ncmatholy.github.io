Rules & scoring

NC(J)MO follows a BAMO-style proof-based format. Both divisions have five problems and a three-hour time limit.

7 points per problem

Solutions are graded on completeness, clarity, and correctness. With five problems, the maximum score is 35 points.

Show your reasoning

Write proofs that explain how you reached your conclusions. Submit any progress you have, even if you have not fully solved a problem.

Allowed tools

You may use writing utensils, erasers, and scratch paper. A straightedge or ruler and a compass are optional.

See the original scoring instructions in the 2025 NCJMO paper and 2025 NCMO paper.

Why this contest?

When you hear the phrase “math competition,” you’ll probably think of a fast-paced, high-pressure short-answer test that rewards accuracy, intuition, and speed. Although that has its own excitement, there is also something special about being able to forget about time and pressure and simply wrestle with a difficult problem: staring out the window as you let your mind wander through the craziest ideas before a solution clicks.

We have met so many of you who are creative and ingenious, and can think deeply and rigorously about difficult problems, but don’t quite thrive in fast-paced, high-pressure environments. Although competitions like the USA(J)MO encourage deep and rigorous thinking, virtually all of these are only accessible at the highest levels, standing behind the walls of short-answer tests such as the AMCs and AIME. Our mission is to make these proof-based olympiads accessible to the community.

Choose your division

NCJMO (Junior)

If you haven’t quite explored beyond school math, or tasted competition math beyond the AMCs, we encourage you to take the NCJMO.

The difficulty is similar to BAMO-8. Explore past NCJMO problems to see what to expect.

NCMO (Olympiad)

If you’ve comfortably qualified for the AIME or have experience with proof writing, such as from an advanced math course, you should challenge yourself with the NCMO.

The difficulty is approximately USAJMO level, with a couple of easier problems at the beginning and potentially a particularly challenging one at the end.

Common questions

Proofs seem hard! Should I take the NC(J)MO?

First things first, don’t be intimidated by the word “proof”! Think of it as a fancy way of saying, “explain your thought process as clearly as you can.” We encourage you to take the NC(J)MO even if you’re inexperienced!

Does NC(J)MO have the same difficulty as USA(J)MO?

NCJMO is similar in difficulty to BAMO-8. NCMO is approximately USAJMO level, with a couple of easier problems at the beginning and potentially a particularly challenging one at the end.
